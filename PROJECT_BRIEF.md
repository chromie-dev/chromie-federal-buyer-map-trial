# Project brief

## User problem

A list of names is not a buyer map. Contractors need to know which organization owns the mission, which office controls the requirement, which contracting activity executes it, which incumbent has history, and what public evidence supports an outreach path.

## Product hypothesis

Chromie can turn agency structure, budgets, forecasts, notices, and awards into a navigable buying graph that explains how a requirement travels from mission intent to acquisition.

## In scope

- A compact graph ontology.
- Evidence-backed nodes and edges.
- Entity resolution across public records.
- Buyer-path ranking for one agency and one capability theme.
- Contradiction and staleness reporting.

## Out of scope

- Personal contact enrichment or private information.
- Automated outreach.
- Production CRM writes.
- Mapping the entire federal government.
- Claiming a named person has authority without direct evidence.

## Questions the final presentation must answer

1. Which public signals best reveal the true requirement owner?
2. How should buyer paths be ranked without hallucinating authority?
3. Where are agency identifiers inconsistent?
4. How should this graph surface inside Chromie's opportunity workflow?
